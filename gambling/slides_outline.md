## Section: How They Keep You Playing

### 15. You run a casino

Groups, two minutes:

**Nobody is allowed to leave. How do you keep them playing?**

(No locked doors.)

NOTE: New activity. Their guesses are then checked against the six tricks that follow. Groups get a point for each trick they predicted.

### 16. Trick 1: Almost!

Image: slot reels showing 7-7-cherry (TikZ).

- You lose, but **nearly** win
- Your brain reacts like winning
- You want to play **more**

Source: Clark et al., *Neuron* (2009).

NOTE: In the study, near-misses felt worse than clear losses but made people want to continue, when they had chosen for themselves. A later review (Barton et al. 2017) was more cautious about near-misses, so keep it at "in one brain study".

### 17. Trick 2: Win or loss?

You bet **75**. The machine flashes, sings and pays **30**.

**Did you win?**

Reveal on click: You **lost 45**. Players remember it as winning.

Source: Barton et al., *Journal of Gambling Studies* (2017).

### 18. Trick 3: "Free" money

- "Bet $5, get $200!"
- It's **credit**, not cash
- Cashing out takes more betting
- Losing streak? Another bonus

NOTE: Generic example, not a specific company's offer. The terms vary by offer; the second and third bullets are from memory.

### 19. Trick 4: Your phone calls you

**One customer says DraftKings sent ___ messages in one month.**

Reveal on click: **about 70** (lawsuit, 2026)

- Emails, texts, notifications
- DraftKings **denies** using AI to target losses or problem gamblers

Source: Boston Globe (Oct. 2026).

NOTE: An allegation from a proposed class action (Vest v. DraftKings), not proven. I kept the company's denial on the slide.

### 20. Trick 5: Your new best friend

- Lose a lot → become a **VIP**
- A personal "host" texts you
- Gifts, hotels, Super Bowl tickets
- Lawsuit: texts continued **after** self-exclusion

Source: ESPN (March 2026). Allegations; no ruling reported.

NOTE: From Sage and Thompson v. FanDuel, DraftKings, NFL et al. The two plaintiffs say they lost over $2 million together. The sportsbooks have asked the court to dismiss it.

### 21. Trick 6: Faster, bigger, longer

- Bet on the **next** play
- Combine bets: a "parlay"
- Huge prize, terrible odds

Bar chart, what the bookmaker keeps: roulette 2.7%, all US sports bets about 10%, parlays in New Jersey **18.7%**.

Source: AGA (2025); InGame (2025, year to date).

### 25. When is it an addiction?

Four or more in a year:

- Needs bigger and bigger bets
- **Can't stop**, restless without it
- **Chases losses**
- Lies about it
- Loses friends, school, job

Source: DSM-5 (nine criteria, shortened), via Gesundheit Österreich.

### 26. It's a brain thing

- Same category as drug addiction
- **Dopamine**: excitement *before* the result
- Unpredictable rewards hook us
- Like scrolling, like loot boxes
- Not weakness, not stupidity

NOTE: The DSM-5 lists it with substance addictions (checked). The dopamine and unpredictable-reward bullets are standard psychology, from memory.
