# Park Ranger Quiz: America's National Parks

How to edit this file: each `###` is a slide. `NOTE:` lines are for Noah and won't appear on slides. `Source:` lines become the tiny source text. Delete a slide to drop it, and rewrite anything you like.

NOTE: How the game runs. Students play in teams of 3 or 4 as "junior park rangers" and keep their own score. Every park is one round of **three slides**: (1) a full-screen photo with a map of the USA and four dots, "Where is this?" (A/B/C/D, 1 point); (2) the park in four short bullets; (3) one guess of a different kind each round (a number, a true/false, a "what happens next"). So they guess twice per park and never listen for more than a minute.

NOTE: Timing. 7 rounds at about 4 minutes each, plus about 12 minutes for Timothy Treadwell, is a full 45 minutes. If you want breathing room, cut Round 5 (Glacier) or Round 7 (Denali) first.

NOTE: Two corrections to your outline, both turned into game material:
1. **Timothy Treadwell was not in Denali.** He lived and died in **Katmai National Park**, also in Alaska, about 500 km south-west of Denali. I kept Denali as a short round and made Katmai the finale.
2. **Mount Rushmore is not a national park.** It is a National Memorial. I made that the trick question of Round 6.

NOTE: Slides follow the 5x5 rule, and every round opens with a full-screen photo.

### 9. Grand Canyon

- Carved by the Colorado River
- 446 km long
- Up to 1.8 km deep
- That is 13 Stephansdoms, stacked

Image: Colorado River at the bottom of the canyon (or a small TikZ stack of 13 cathedral towers next to the canyon depth, if it looks clean).

NOTE: 1,857 m deep ÷ 136 m (Stephansdom south tower) = 13.6.

### 10. Guess: deaths

How many people die in the Grand Canyon in a typical year?

Closest team gets the point.

Reveal on click: **about 12.** More than in any other national park.

Source: NPS data, 2010–2020 (134 deaths), via Outforia.

NOTE: You suggested guessing deaths; this is the one park where the number is solid enough to use. The 134 figure covers 2010–2020 and comes from an FOIA request reported by several outlets; other counts for other years land in the same range. Per visitor, the Grand Canyon is not especially dangerous (about 4.4 million visits a year), which is worth saying out loud.

Reveal on click: The park's glaciers have shrunk **39%** on average since 1966.

NOTE: I avoided the popular line "150 glaciers in 1850, 26 today". The 26 (in 2015) is solid; the 150 is an old estimate, and USGS now says about 80. The photo pair makes the point without the shaky number. Link to Austria if you like: the Pasterze under the Großglockner is losing tens of metres a year too.

### 21. True or false?

"Mount Rushmore is a national park."

Reveal on click: **False.** It is a National **Memorial**.

- Built 1927 to 1941
- Each head: 18 m tall
- Carved into the Black Hills
- Sacred land of the Lakota

NOTE: One sentence you can add out loud: the Black Hills were promised to the Lakota by treaty in 1868 and taken after gold was found. In 1980 the Supreme Court awarded compensation; the Lakota have refused the money and want the land back. I kept it to one bullet on the slide; tell me if you want a full slide on it.

### 23. Denali

- North America's highest mountain
- 6,190 m
- Großglockner: 3,798 m
- Name fight: Denali or Mount McKinley?

Image: simple TikZ silhouette comparison, Großglockner next to Denali.

NOTE: The name. "Denali" is the Koyukon Athabaskan name. The US government called the mountain Mount McKinley from 1917, Obama changed it to Denali in 2015, and Trump changed it back to Mount McKinley in January 2025. The **park** is still called Denali National Park, and Alaska's two Republican senators want the mountain's name changed back. I kept this to one neutral bullet.
