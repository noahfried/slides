# Thanksgiving: teacher briefing

Read this before class. It follows the slides in order. **Bold** = good to say out loud. Timing is for a 50-minute lesson.

---

## 1. Title (turkey) — 1 min

Let the plain turkey sit there. If anyone asks: Americans eat about **30 million turkeys** at Thanksgiving (National Turkey Federation, 2025).

## 2. What are you grateful for? — 5 min

Go around the room. You answer first to set the tone.

## 3. Why do we celebrate it? — 5 min

- **Autumn 1621, Plymouth colony** (today's Massachusetts). The Pilgrims had survived their first winter; about half of them had died.
- Wampanoag leader **Massasoit came with about 90 men**. Everyone feasted for **three days**.
- Food we know about: **five deer** the Wampanoag brought, wild birds, probably fish and corn. Turkey is possible but not recorded. **No pie**: they had no butter, wheat flour or sugar.
- It wasn't called "Thanksgiving" at the time. It was a harvest festival.
- **Abraham Lincoln** made it a national holiday in **1863**, during the Civil War. Since **1941** it has been the **fourth Thursday of November** (this year, today: Nov. 26).
- Not everyone celebrates: since 1970 some Native Americans have held a **National Day of Mourning** in Plymouth on the same day.

About the painting: Jennie Brownscombe painted it about 300 years after the event, which is a good question to ask the class. She painted two versions (1914 and 1925). The 1914 one was criticized for showing **Plains-style feather headdresses** (from the Great Plains, not New England) and a **log cabin** (not how the Pilgrims built). Your file says 1925, but it seems to show both, so it may really be the 1914 one. Fun question: **"What's wrong with this picture?"**

Sources: Edward Winslow's letter (1621), quoted on Wikipedia; National Museum of Women in the Arts.

## 4. Macy's Parade — 3 min

- New York City, every Thanksgiving morning since **1924**. The first parade had **animals from the Central Park Zoo**; balloons arrived in **1927**.
- Giant balloons need about **90 handlers** each. Good quick guess: "How many people hold one balloon?"
- It was cancelled only during World War II (1942–44), because the balloon materials were needed for the war effort.
- **This year is the 100th parade, and it was postponed for the first time ever**, to **Saturday, Nov. 28**, because of gusts of 50–60 mph. Balloons can't fly in strong wind: in 1997 the Cat in the Hat balloon hit a lamppost and badly injured a spectator.
- About **34 million** people watched it on TV last year, a record.

Sources: Wikipedia; CNN and NBC New York (Nov. 25, 2026); TheWrap (viewers, 2025).

## 5. Football — 3 min

- NFL games on Thanksgiving are a tradition: the **Detroit Lions** have played on Thanksgiving every year since 1945 (and in 1934–38), the **Dallas Cowboys** since 1966.
- **Today's games (US time):** Bears at Lions (1 pm ET), Eagles at Cowboys (4:30 pm), Chiefs at Bills (8:20 pm). In Austria, that's 19:00, 22:30 and 02:20.
- New this year: a game the night before, on **Thanksgiving Eve** (Packers at Rams), for the first time.
- The typical family: eat, then fall asleep on the sofa in front of the game.

Sources: CBS Sports (2026 schedule); Wikipedia (history). The Lions and Cowboys years are from a page summary; worth checking if anyone asks.

## 6. Weight gain guess — 2 min

- Ask for guesses in kilos. Many people think it's **2–3 kg** (the American myth is "5 pounds").
- Real answer: **0.37 kg on average**, from Thanksgiving to New Year (a study of 195 adults).
- The catch: **people don't lose it again**, so over the years it adds up. Heavier people gained more, and 14% gained over 2.3 kg.
- Fun fact: participants thought they'd gained much more than they had, overestimating by about 1.5 kg.

Sources: Yanovski et al., *NEJM* (2000); NICHD.

## 7–10. Thanksgiving across America — 6 min

Show the photo, let them guess the region, then click.

- **Maryland: sauerkraut with turkey.** Baltimore had many **German immigrants**, who brought sauerkraut to the Thanksgiving table. A good hook for Austrians. (The sauerkraut tradition is from the sources; the German-immigrant reason is from memory.)
- **Hawaii: kalua turkey.** Smoked or slow-roasted Hawaiian-style, sometimes with **poke** or sashimi on the side.
- **The South: pecan pie.** Pumpkin pie wins nationally, but Texas, Louisiana and Mississippi prefer pecan. Sweet potato pie is big in Georgia, North Carolina and Maryland.
- **Stuffing vs. dressing.** Same dish (bread, onions, celery, herbs), different name. About three-quarters of Americans say **stuffing**; the South says **dressing**. One explanation: stuffing is cooked inside the bird, dressing in a separate dish. Southern dressing is often made with **cornbread**.

Sources: Lonely Planet; The Takeout; CBS Texas (GE Appliances survey); Mental Floss / Instacart (pies).

## 11–16. Chopped! — about 20 min

**What it is:** a US TV cooking competition on Food Network, running since 2009. Four chefs get a **mystery basket** of strange ingredients and must use **all of them**. If the judges don't like your dish, you're "chopped" (eliminated).

**How to run it:**
1. Four teams. Reveal each team's basket (slides 12–15). Let them react; it's half the fun.
2. Slide 16 shows all baskets while they work.
3. **10 minutes** to plan **one dish** that uses all four ingredients. They need: a **name** for the dish, how they'd **cook** it (steps), and how they'd **plate** it. They can draw it.
4. **1 minute per team** to pitch it, like a TV chef. Encourage cooking vocabulary: roast, glaze, fold, garnish, crisp, drizzle.
5. Judges: you, plus one person from each other team. Or the class votes (no voting for your own team).
6. The loser is "**chopped**", dramatically.

**If you have extra time:** second round, where teams swap baskets for dessert. Or ask each team what an **Austrian** Thanksgiving would look like.

**The baskets:**
| Team | Thanksgiving | Thanksgiving | Weird | Wild card |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Turkey | Canned cranberry sauce | Marshmallows | Pumpkin seed oil |
| 2 | Sweet potatoes | Pecans | Candy corn | Sauerkraut |
| 3 | Pumpkin | Cornbread | Hot sauce | Wafers |
| 4 | Green beans | Crispy fried onions | Maple syrup | Coca-Cola |

**What the American items are**, in case students ask:
- **Canned cranberry sauce**: a jelly that slides out of the can in one piece, ridges and all, served in slices.
- **Marshmallows**: Americans put them on sweet potatoes at Thanksgiving, as a side dish.
- **Candy corn**: small sugary candies shaped like corn kernels, really a Halloween thing.
- **Cornbread**: sweet-ish bread made from cornmeal, popular in the South.
- **Crispy fried onions**: from a can, the topping on green bean casserole (a 1955 Campbell's recipe).

Sources: NPR (Chopped format); Campbell's.

## 17. Happy Thanksgiving! — closing

Happy Thanksgiving! 🦃
