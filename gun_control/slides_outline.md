## Section: Basic Info

### 10. How many guns?
- About **393 million** civilian guns in 2017 (Small Arms Survey)
- Industry-based estimates today: **500 million or more**
- More guns than people, but ownership is concentrated:
  - **32%** of adults personally own a gun
  - About **4 in 10** live in a household with one
  - Main reason owners give: **protection**
- Source: Small Arms Survey 2018; Pew Research Center, June 2023

### 11. The Second Amendment (1791)
- Quote box: *"A well regulated Militia, being necessary to the security of a free State, the right of the people to keep and bear Arms, shall not be infringed."*
- What does it protect? A **militia's** right, or an **individual's** right?
- Supreme Court:
  - *Heller* (2008): an individual right to keep a handgun at home
  - *Bruen* (2022): a right to carry a gun in public, and gun laws must fit America's "historical tradition"

### 12. What's legal, what's not?
- **Legal nationwide:** handguns, hunting rifles, shotguns, and semi-automatic rifles such as the AR-15 (in most states)
- **Nearly impossible:** new fully automatic machine guns (only those registered before 1986 can be sold to civilians)
- **Banned in some states:** "assault weapons" and high-capacity magazines (about 10 states + DC, including New York and California)
- **Recently loosened:** the federal tax on silencers and short-barreled rifles dropped to **$0** in January 2026

### 13. Who can buy a gun?
- Licensed dealers must run a **federal background check** (about 2 minutes, by phone or online)
- Banned buyers include people convicted of felonies or domestic violence and people involuntarily committed for mental illness
- **Private sales** (between individuals) need no check under federal law; about 20 states require one
- Age: **18** for rifles and shotguns; **21** for handguns from a dealer (some states set 21 for everything)
- **No federal gun registry** and no federal waiting period

### 14. Carrying a gun in public
- **29 states**: "permitless" or "constitutional" carry, so no license is needed
- Other states require a permit, sometimes with training
- Most states also allow **open carry** (visible, e.g. on the hip)
- Source: count as of 2026

### 15. Shooting ranges
- Roughly **8,000+** shooting ranges in the US (indoor and outdoor)
- You can usually **rent a gun** at the range, often without owning one or having a license
- Popular with tourists: Las Vegas ranges advertise machine-gun "experiences"
- Source: IBISWorld 2025 (8,289 businesses)


### 16. Compare: the USA and Austria
| | USA | Austria |
|---|---|---|

| Minimum age, handgun | 21 (dealer) | 25 (since 2026) |

### 19. My experience

  - One student was passionate about gun rights, and she was a loser / very weird 🤓

NOTE: Utah has allowed campus carry by law since 2004; about 10 states require public universities to allow it. Utah campus carry has been in the news since the September 2025 shooting of Charlie Kirk at Utah Valley University. Students may bring it up.

### 20. Guns follow party lines
- **45%** of Republicans own a gun vs. **20%** of Democrats
- Teachers carrying guns at school: **74%** of Republicans in favor vs. **27%** of Democrats
- In the Senate, Democrats almost always get an **"F"** from the NRA and Republicans an **"A"**
- Last major federal gun law (2022): **all 50** Democratic senators voted yes, but only **15 of 50** Republicans
- Source: Pew Research Center, June 2023; Senate vote, 23 June 2022

### 21. New York City: legal, but…
- In NYC, **concealed carry is legal** with a license
  - i.e. you may carry a gun in public, hidden from view
  - Easier to get since *Bruen* (2022) struck down New York's old "proper cause" rule
- But the culture strongly tilts against it:
  - I've never seen anyone except a police officer carry a gun in the city
  - If I did, I would panic and probably assume the person was crazy 
  - Carrying is banned on the **subway**, in **Times Square**, and in many other "sensitive places"

## Section: School Shootings

### 25. How frequent are they?
- **2026 so far:** 27 school shootings where someone was hurt or killed (as of 19 Oct.; 17 killed, 29 injured)
- Counting *any* gunfire on K-12 and college campuses: **52+** (as of 15 Oct.)
- Most school years since 2021 have had **100+** incidents of gunfire on school grounds
- The numbers depend on **how you count**!
- Source: Education Week tracker; CNN; Everytown (144 incidents in 2023–24)

### 27. Putting it in perspective
- Bar chart, deaths per year in the US:
  - Gun deaths (2024): **44,447** (~120 per day)
    - 62% suicides, 35% homicides
  - Traffic deaths (2024): **39,254** (~107 per day)
  - Killed in school shootings (2026 so far): **17**
- Bullets:
  - For any one student, being shot at school is **extremely rare** (~50 million public school students)
  - But guns are the **leading cause of death** for American children and teens (ages 1–19), ahead of car crashes (since 2020)
- Source: CDC (via Pew, 2026); NHTSA; Education Week; CDC WONDER

### 28. What are we doing about it? (1) Trying to understand it
- Possible explanations people offer:
  - Violent video games
  - Social media
  - Family dysfunction
  - Easy access to firearms
  - Mental illness
- (Optional reveal) Which of these do you think Americans argue about most? Why?

### 29. What are we doing about it? (2) Arguing about solutions
- **The public** (Gallup, Oct. 2025): 60% want stricter gun laws, 27% keep as now, 12% less strict
- Almost everyone agrees on some things:
  - Stopping people with mental illness from buying guns: **88%** of Republicans, **89%** of Democrats
- Strong disagreement on others:
  - Banning assault-style weapons: **85%** of Democrats in favor; a **majority** of Republicans opposed
- Source: Gallup; Pew Research Center, June 2023

### 30. What are we doing about it? (3) Politicians
- **2022:** Bipartisan Safer Communities Act, the first major federal gun law in ~30 years (after the Uvalde shooting)
  - Tougher background checks for buyers under 21, money for "red flag" laws and school mental health
- **2025–26:** The Trump administration has loosened federal rules
  - $0 tax on silencers; rollback of Biden-era rules on gun-show and private sales
  - Supporters: restoring Second Amendment rights. Critics: making guns easier to misuse
- **States** are moving in opposite directions: more permitless carry in red states, more restrictions in blue states
