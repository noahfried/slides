## Section: Basic Info

### 4. NYC schools by the numbers
- **About 885,000** students in NYC Public Schools (3-K to 12th grade, 2025–26)
- **Plus about 151,000** in public **charter** schools (publicly funded, privately run)
- **1,599** schools run by NYC Public Schools
- **Roughly 200,000** in private schools, most of them religious (Jewish and Catholic)
- About **73%** of public school students live in poverty; **14%** are homeless or in temporary housing
- Source: NYCPS Data at a Glance 2025–26; NYC Comptroller, Oct. 2026

NOTE: Your "~1M public students" is right if you count charter schools (885K + 151K ≈ 1.04M). I split them because charters are a big topic in NYC. The private school number is my estimate: I couldn't find an official 2025–26 citywide total. The last official count I found was 241,900 (2012–13), and the Comptroller says private schools lost about 6,800 students since 2020–21. "Roughly 200,000" is reasonable, but I'd keep the word "roughly."

### 9. Statement slide
- (statement) **For NYC students, the application process begins in pre-K.**

### 10. Applying to elementary school
- Every 5-year-old in NYC is **guaranteed a kindergarten seat**, but not at a specific school
- Families rank schools; if a school has more applicants than seats, there's a **lottery**
- Who gets priority (in order):
  1. Live in the school's **zone** and have a **sibling** there
  2. Live in the school's **zone**
  3. Have a **sibling** at the school (but live outside the zone)
  4. Attend the school's **pre-K**
- Source: NYC Public Schools, kindergarten admissions (fall 2027 cycle)

NOTE: Your order was sibling → pre-K → zone. The official order for zoned schools puts the zone first (zone + sibling, then zone, then out-of-zone siblings, then pre-K students). I simplified the eight official groups to the four that matter. Most zoned schools take everyone in their zone, so the lottery mostly matters for popular and non-zoned schools.

### 11. Gifted and Talented (G&T)
- Separate, accelerated classes, starting in **kindergarten**
- About **2,500** kindergarten seats a year; about 18,000 G&T students in total
- How do 4-year-olds get in?
  - Public pre-K students: their **pre-K teacher** evaluates them
  - Private pre-K or no pre-K: an **interview** with the Education Department
  - Eligible children then enter a **lottery**
- Until 2021, children took a **test** at age 4
- **Mayor Mamdani** wants to stop admitting kindergartners to G&T; for now the program continues (fall 2027 applications open Dec. 2026)
- Source: NYC Public Schools; AP / news reports, 2025–26

NOTE: Your outline said "Intellectual/'talent' evaluation." There's been no test since 2021: it's now a pre-K teacher evaluation (or interview), then a lottery. I added Mamdani's proposal because it's current and parents in NYC are arguing about it right now. Supporters say it ends testing 4-year-olds and segregating classes early; critics say it pushes families to private schools. I kept the slide neutral. Check before class whether the city has announced a final decision.

### 12. Elementary school
- School day: about **8:30 a.m. to 3:00 p.m.** (about 6 hours 20 minutes; times vary by school)
- One main teacher, one classroom, the same classmates all day
- Subjects: Math, English Language Arts (ELA), Science, Social Studies, Spanish, Music, Technology
- After **5th grade** (age 10–11): you must **apply** to middle school

NOTE: I added "one main teacher…" as a parallel to the Volksschule. Delete if that wasn't your experience.

### 13. Applying to middle school
- Apply in the fall of 5th grade and rank schools in order of preference
- Some schools use a **lottery** with priorities (e.g. living in the district)
- Others **screen** students using **4th-grade report card grades**
- This is when many families **leave** the public schools: the biggest drop in enrollment happens between 5th and 6th grade
- Example: my middle school, **MS 54 Booker T. Washington** (Upper West Side): ms54.org/applications
- Source: NYC Public Schools; NYC Comptroller, Oct. 2026

NOTE: I couldn't open ms54.org (the connection was refused), so I haven't checked that the link works. MS 54's site says it has about 840 students. The 5th→6th drop is from the Comptroller's October 2026 report; families switch to private, charter, or suburban schools at that point.

## Section: Applying to High School

### 15. Three separate applications
- **1. Specialized High Schools** (8 schools): admission by **one test only**, the SHSAT
  - e.g. Stuyvesant, Bronx Science, Brooklyn Tech
- **2. LaGuardia High School** (performing and visual arts): admission by **audition** or portfolio
- **3. The main application:** 700+ programs at 400+ high schools
  - **Screened** programs: grades, attendance, sometimes interviews
  - **Audition** programs, e.g. Frank Sinatra School of the Arts
  - **Open** programs: lottery
- Each application can give you **at most one offer**
- Source: NYC Public Schools, high school admissions

NOTE: Your three types were Specialized / Screened / Performing arts (LaGuardia + Frank Sinatra). The actual split is by *application*: Frank Sinatra is an audition program inside the main application, not a separate category, and screened schools are part of the main application too. I reorganized it so "at most one offer from each" is accurate. Put your version back if you prefer the simpler picture.

### 16. Happening right now
- This month, about **26,000** NYC 8th graders take the **SHSAT**
  - Test days: **Nov. 14/15 and 21, 2026**
- High school applications are due **Dec. 4, 2026**
- Offers arrive **March 3, 2027**
- Source: NYC Public Schools, fall 2027 admissions calendar

NOTE: This is an addition. It's a good hook: kids their age in NYC are cramming for this test the same weeks as your lesson.

### 17. The SHSAT
- **3 hours**, 114 multiple-choice and fill-in questions, no calculator
- English (reading, editing) and Math
- **Your score is the only thing that counts.** No grades, no interviews, no essays
- About **26,100** students took it; about **4,000** got an offer (about **1 in 6**)
- Lowest score that got into Stuyvesant: **561** (2026 admissions)
- Source: NYC Public Schools; Chalkbeat, July 2026

### 18. Who gets in?
- Bar chart, share of offers to the eight test schools (fall 2026):
  - Asian American **56.5%**
  - White **23.5%**
  - Latino **6.5%**
  - Black **3.5%**
- Stuyvesant offered seats to **3** Black students
- Supporters of the test: it's **objective**, the same rules for everyone
- Critics: it favors families who can pay for **test prep** and leaves out Black and Latino students
- Mayor Mamdani has said he will **keep** the test and study it for bias
- Source: Chalkbeat, July 2026

NOTE: This is an addition, and it's the biggest political fight about NYC schools. I kept it neutral (supporters/critics). For comparison: Black students are about 19% of NYC public school students (Comptroller), and Latino students about 40% (from memory, not verified; I'd check before adding it to the slide). Delete the slide if you'd rather not get into race.

### 19. No guarantee
- Applying to all three is **far from a guarantee**
- At my middle school, about **30 students** got **no** high school offer at all in the first round
- There's usually **no "default" school**: most NYC high schools aren't zoned
- Students without an offer go on **waitlists** and are placed later at a school with open seats
- Citywide (2024): about **3%** of applicants got no offer to any school on their list
- Source: NYC Public Schools, 2024 admissions data

NOTE: I added "in the first round" and the waitlist bullet: every student does eventually get a seat, so "not admitted to any high school" could mislead. Also, a few zoned high schools still exist (mostly in Queens and Staten Island), hence "usually no default." The 3% is for 2024, the latest citywide number I found.

- Specialized schools: very competitive, lots of homework, many AP courses
- Small schools: themes like law, health, technology, or the arts

## Section: Compare

### 23. Sorting students: NYC vs. Austria
| | NYC | Austria |
|---|---|---|
| First selection | G&T at **4–5** | Gymnasium (AHS) or Mittelschule at **10** |
| Middle school | Apply at **10–11** (lottery or grades) | Based on Volksschule grades |
| High school | Apply at **13–14**; one test for the top 8 schools | Oberstufe, BHS (HTL, HAK…) or apprenticeship at **14** |
| Private schools | Many, up to **$70,000+** a year | Mostly Catholic, far cheaper |
| University | Applications, essays, tests; under 5% at top schools | Matura, then mostly **open** admission; free for EU students (within standard study time) |

- **Think:** Austria is often criticized for sorting kids at 10. Is New York better or worse?
- Source: NYC Public Schools; oesterreich.gv.at; studium.at

NOTE: This is an addition. Austrian students argue about tracking at 10 all the time, so this is a natural discussion prompt. "Far cheaper" for Austrian private schools is deliberately vague: I didn't verify a typical fee. Austrian university details: free within standard study time + 2 semesters for EU citizens, then €363.36/semester; some programs (medicine, psychology) have entrance exams.
