## Section: Output Practice

### 20. You are the new senator
- Each table is one state
- Something goes wrong in America
- Your state votes: **yes** or **no**?
- Tell us why

### 21. Your state at a glance
- Six small photo tiles (the same photos as the profile slides), each with the state name and three or four words:
  - **Maine:** old, rural
  - **North Carolina:** swing state, growing
  - **Texas:** border, oil, 40% Hispanic
  - **Iowa:** farmers, diesel
  - **South Carolina:** conservative, 26% Black
  - **Alaska:** oil pays for everything

NOTE: This is the cheat sheet students look at while they argue. I can leave it on screen under each scenario instead of giving it its own slide; say which you prefer.

### 22. Scenario 1: the fire
- A huge fire destroys **half of America's oil**
- Petrol now costs three times as much
- **Do you vote to buy oil from Russia?**

NOTE: Your scenario, lightly tidied. Expect Iowa (diesel) and Maine (heating oil, old people on fixed incomes) to say yes, and Texas and Alaska to say no, because expensive oil is good for them. That split is the fun of it.

### 23. Scenario 2: the robots
- A company invents a **robot farmer**
- Food gets cheaper. Farm workers lose their jobs
- **Do you vote to ban the robot?**

### 24. Scenario 3: the birthday
- Americans now live to **100**
- The pension money is running out
- **Do you vote to raise the retirement age to 80?**

NOTE: Scenarios 2 and 3 are mine, written to match yours: one silly premise, one real trade-off, one yes/no vote. The robot splits Iowa (farm owners vs. farm workers) and Texas (many farm workers are immigrants); the birthday is aimed at Maine, the oldest state. Replace or cut either one.
