# Higher or Lower: America in 10 Numbers

How to edit this file: each `###` is a slide. `NOTE:` lines are for Noah and won't appear on slides. `Source:` lines become the tiny source text. Delete a slide to drop it, and rewrite anything you like.

NOTE: How the game runs. It is a chain, like the real game: the answer from one round becomes the number to beat in the next. Each round is **two slides**: a guess slide (the reveal appears on click, on the same slide) and one lecture slide about the new item. That gives 11 items, 10 rounds and 24 slides in total.

NOTE: The answers go L, L, H, L, H, L, H, L, H, H. Rounds 1, 4 and 7 are close calls. Rounds 6, 8 and 10 are easy, so they work as quick breathers.

NOTE: I started the chain with **Eurovision 2026 in Vienna** so the first number is one your students already have a feel for. If you'd rather open with your own example (Super Bowl vs. turkeys), delete round 1 and I'll make the Super Bowl the starting number, with a short Super Bowl slide before round 2.

NOTE: Units are mixed on purpose (viewers, turkeys, dollars). The guess slides always say what is being counted.

- Halftime show: Bad Bunny (128 million viewers)
- Americans ate about **1.48 billion** chicken wings that day
- In Austria it starts at 00:30 on Monday morning

### 6. Thanksgiving

- Fourth Thursday in November: this year, **November 26**
- The story: Pilgrims and Wampanoag share a harvest meal, 1621
- A national holiday since 1863 (Abraham Lincoln)
- Turkey, stuffing, cranberry sauce, pumpkin pie
- The President "pardons" a turkey every year
- The next day is Black Friday

Image: a Thanksgiving dinner table or the turkey pardon (public domain, White House).

- The classic American holiday: the road trip
- Austria has 6 national parks

- McDonald's started in California in 1940
- Ray Kroc turned it into a franchise from 1955
- About 13,800 in the USA; about 200 in Austria

- Free refills, huge portions, drive-throughs
- Other giants: Subway, Starbucks, Chick-fil-A, Taco Bell
